# Quadratic equations: four ways to solve them

## What makes an equation quadratic

A quadratic equation can be written in standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are real numbers and $a \neq 0$. The highest power of the variable is 2. A quadratic equation has at most two real solutions, also called roots.

## Method 1: Extracting square roots

Use this method when there is no $x$ term, that is, when $b = 0$. Isolate $x^2$, then take the square root of both sides. Remember that a positive number has two square roots.

Example: $x^2 - 49 = 0$ gives $x^2 = 49$, so $x = 7$ or $x = -7$.

## Method 2: Factoring

Write the equation in standard form, factor the left side, then use the zero product property: if $pq = 0$, then $p = 0$ or $q = 0$.

Example: $x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0$ factors as $(x + 2)(x + 3) = 0$, so $x = -2$ or $x = -3$.

Always check by substituting. For $x = -2$: $(-2)^2 + 5(-2) + 6 = 4 - 10 + 6 = 0$.

## Method 3: Completing the square

Rewrite the equation so that one side is a perfect square trinomial.

Example: $x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0$. Move the constant term: $x^2 + 6x = 7$. Add the square of half the coefficient of $x$, which is $3^2 = 9$, to both sides: $x^2 + 6x + 9 = 16$, so $(x + 3)^2 = 16$. Then $x + 3 = 4$ or $x + 3 = -4$, which gives $x = 1$ or $x = -7$.

## Method 4: The quadratic formula

The quadratic formula works for every quadratic equation in standard form:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

Example: $2x^2 - 3x - 2 = 0$ has $a = 2$, $b = -3$ and $c = -2$. Then $b^2 - 4ac = 9 + 16 = 25$, so $x = \frac{3 \pm 5}{4}$, which gives $x = 2$ or $x = -\frac{1}{2}$.

## The discriminant

The expression $b^2 - 4ac$ is called the discriminant. It tells you the nature of the roots before you solve:

- If $b^2 - 4ac > 0$, there are two distinct real roots.
- If $b^2 - 4ac = 0$, there is exactly one real root, called a repeated root.
- If $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, there are no real roots.

## Choosing a method

Extracting square roots is quickest when $b = 0$. Factoring is efficient when the trinomial factors easily over the integers. Completing the square shows where the quadratic formula comes from and helps you write a quadratic function in vertex form. The quadratic formula always works, so use it when the other methods are awkward or when the roots are not rational.
